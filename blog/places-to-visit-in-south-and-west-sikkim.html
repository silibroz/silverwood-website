Places to Visit in South and West Sikkim: Complete Guide (2026)

West and South Sikkim are the calmer, greener side of the state. Think old monasteries and ruined palaces in Pelling, a sacred lake hidden in forest, giant statues in Namchi and Ravangla, and tea gardens rolling down the hills. If you are planning a trip beyond Gangtok, this guide covers the best places to visit in South and West Sikkim, with distances, highlights and practical tips.

South and West Sikkim at a glance

  • West Sikkim base: Pelling, close to monasteries, Rabdentse, the Skywalk and Khecheopalri Lake.
  • South Sikkim base: Namchi and Ravangla, known for large statues, tea and hill treks.
  • Best months: March to June and October to November.
  • Time needed: about 3 days for West Sikkim and 2 days for South Sikkim.

Best places to visit in West Sikkim

1. Pemayangtse Monastery

One of the oldest monasteries in Sikkim, Pemayangtse sits on a hilltop about 2 km from Pelling. Its name means "perfect sublime lotus". It overlooks the ruins of Rabdentse and has broad views across the valley. Allow an hour.

2. Rabdentse Ruins

About 3 km from Pelling, Rabdentse was Sikkim's second capital until 1814. The ruins of the old palace are being restored, and a short walk through forest leads to the site. It is a quiet spot with views of the hills.

3. Pelling Skywalk and Chenrezig Statue

The Skywalk is a glass walkway at around 7,200 ft, with a view straight down to the valley floor. It is close to a large Chenrezig statue near Sangachoeling Monastery, which was founded in the 17th century. If heights make you nervous, the statue and the hilltop views are still worth the visit.

4. Sangachoeling Monastery

Founded by Lama Lhatsun Chempo in the 17th century, Sangachoeling is one of the oldest monasteries in Sikkim. It is a short drive from Pelling and combines well with the Skywalk.

5. Khecheopalri Lake

A sacred, wish-fulfilling lake set in forest at around 1,700 m, roughly 25 km from Pelling. Prayer flags hang over the water, and a short walk leads to the lake. Visitors are asked to keep it quiet and clean.

6. Rimbi Falls

A waterfall on the road towards Khecheopalri and Yuksom, near Darap village. It is an easy roadside stop with a short walk.

7. Singshore Bridge

A tall suspension bridge on the Pelling to Khecheopalri road, about 14 km from Pelling, strung across a deep valley. Walk out onto it for a view of the forest far below.

8. Kanchenjunga Falls

A waterfall near Pelling named after Kanchenjunga, the third-highest mountain in the world. It fits easily into a half-day sightseeing loop.

9. Yuksom

Sikkim's first capital, where the first Chogyal was crowned in 1642. Yuksom has the Norbugang Coronation Throne and Dubdi Monastery, and it is the base for the Dzongri and Goecha La treks. It is a longer drive from Pelling, roughly 40 km.

10. Tashiding

A hilltop monastery above the Rathong and Rangit rivers, one of Sikkim's holiest sites. The Bumchu festival is held here in late winter or early spring, and the village is peaceful and scenic.

Best places to visit in South Sikkim

11. Namchi

Namchi, whose name means "sky high", sits at around 1,675 m and is the district headquarters. It is known for two large landmarks:

  • Char Dham (Solophok): a spiritual complex about 5 km from Namchi with replicas of India's four Dhams and a 108 ft Shiva statue. The evening aarti is a highlight.
  • Samdruptse Hill: home to a 135 ft statue of Guru Padmasambhava, and a popular sunrise viewpoint.

12. Ravangla and Buddha Park

Ravangla, at around 2,100 m, is known for the Buddha Park, which has a large seated Buddha statue of about 130 ft, set in landscaped grounds with views of the Himalaya. The town makes a good base for walks and monasteries nearby.

13. Ralang Monastery

One of the largest monasteries in Sikkim, close to Ravangla. It holds a monastery festival in winter and is a calm place to spend an hour.

14. Temi Tea Garden

Sikkim's only tea estate, with terraced slopes of tea running down the hillside. It is a short drive from Namchi and is a favourite for photographs and tea tasting.

15. Tendong Hill

At around 2,640 m, Tendong Hill is sacred to the Lepcha people. A 6 km trek from Damthang leads to the summit, which offers wide views of Kanchenjunga and the Singalila range.

16. Maenam Hill and Maenam Wildlife Sanctuary

Maenam Hill rises to about 3,263 m above Ravangla and is known as the treasure house of medicines. A moderate trek of around four hours leads to the summit and the Maenam Gompa. The sanctuary around it is home to red pandas, Himalayan black bears and many birds.

17. Borong

A small village at around 1,800 m with hot springs near the Rangit River and views of Mount Narsing and Mount Pandim. It is a peaceful stop on the Ravangla side, good for birdwatching and relaxing.

Sample itineraries

West Sikkim: 3 days from Pelling

  1. Day 1: Pemayangtse Monastery, Rabdentse Ruins and sunset at a Kanchenjunga viewpoint.
  2. Day 2: Skywalk and Chenrezig statue, Sangachoeling Monastery, then Rimbi Falls.
  3. Day 3: Singshore Bridge and Khecheopalri Lake, with Yuksom or Tashiding if you have time.

South Sikkim: 2 days

  1. Day 1: Samdruptse sunrise, Char Dham and the Temi Tea Garden.
  2. Day 2: Ravangla Buddha Park and Ralang Monastery, with a hill walk if you have time.

Best time to visit South and West Sikkim

March to June is great for rhododendrons and fresh green hills. October to November brings the clearest skies and the sharpest views of Kanchenjunga, and these months are also good for treks like Tendong and Maenam. Winter days are cool and bright, which suits monasteries and sunrise points.

Practical tips

  • Start early. Mountain views are usually clearest in the morning.
  • Roads are winding, so allow extra time between places and carry motion-sickness tablets if needed.
  • Carry a light jacket year-round, and warmer layers for hill treks.
  • Dress modestly at monasteries and remove shoes where asked.
  • Check opening days and entry timings locally, since they can change.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best places to visit in West Sikkim?

Pemayangtse Monastery, Rabdentse Ruins, the Pelling Skywalk, Khecheopalri Lake, Rimbi Falls, the Singshore Bridge, Yuksom and Tashiding are the highlights of West Sikkim.

What are the best places to visit in South Sikkim?

Namchi (Char Dham and Samdruptse), Ravangla (Buddha Park and Ralang Monastery), Temi Tea Garden, Tendong Hill, Maenam Hill and Borong are the main attractions.

How many days do I need for West Sikkim?

Plan 3 days based in Pelling to cover the monasteries, Rabdentse, Skywalk, Khecheopalri and Rimbi Falls. Add 1 to 2 days if you want to include Yuksom and Tashiding.

How many days do I need for South Sikkim?

Two days are enough for Namchi and Ravangla. Add a day if you want a trek on Tendong Hill or Maenam Hill, or to visit the hot springs at Borong.

What is the best time to visit South and West Sikkim?

March to June and October to November are great for clear views and comfortable weather. Spring brings rhododendrons, and autumn brings the sharpest mountain views.

Is the Pelling Skywalk worth visiting?

Yes. It is a glass walkway with a view far down to the valley, and it sits near a large Chenrezig statue. It is not suitable for anyone with a fear of heights.

Where should I stay in West Sikkim?

Pelling is the best base for West Sikkim because the monasteries, Rabdentse, the Skywalk and Khecheopalri are all within a short drive. Silverwood Pelling is our property there.
