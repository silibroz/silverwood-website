Best Offbeat Destinations in Sikkim: 10 Hidden Gems (2026)

Sikkim is famous for Gangtok, Pelling and North Sikkim, but some of its best moments happen far from the usual circuit: a hairpin road above the clouds, a monastery on a quiet hill, a village where the only traffic is yaks. If you want to skip the crowds, this guide covers the best offbeat destinations in Sikkim, with distances, the best season and permit notes.

Offbeat Sikkim at a glance

  • East Sikkim: Zuluk, Nathang Valley, Aritar and Lampokhari Lake.
  • West Sikkim: Yuksom, Tashiding, Rinchenpong and Khecheopalri Lake.
  • North Sikkim: Dzongu, Thangu Valley and Chopta Valley.
  • Best time: March to June and October to December.

Offbeat destinations in East Sikkim

1. Zuluk (Dzuluk)

Zuluk is a tiny hamlet at roughly 9,400 to 10,000 ft, about 90 km from Gangtok (a 4 to 5 hour drive). It sits on a former trade route between Kalimpong and Tibet and is best known for its dramatic zig-zag road with 32 hairpin bends, and for Thambi View Point, which looks out at Kanchenjunga. It lies within the Pangolakha Wildlife Sanctuary, and rhododendrons bloom on the hills in the warm months. Winter brings snow. A permit is needed, so book through a registered operator.

2. Nathang Valley

Around 13,500 ft and roughly 105 km from Gangtok, Nathang Valley is a high, moon-like landscape with quiet grasslands, a lake nearby at Kupup and a very different feel from Tsomgo. It is a good pick for travellers who want altitude and silence. Best in April to June and October to November, with a permit required.

3. Aritar and Lampokhari Lake

Aritar is a peaceful village about 65 km from Gangtok, known for Lampokhari Lake, a heart-shaped lake with pedal boats, plus a small monastery and orange orchards around. It is an easy, relaxed stop and works well at almost any time of year.

Offbeat destinations in West Sikkim

4. Yuksom

Yuksom is Sikkim's first capital, where the first Chogyal was crowned in 1642. It has the Norbugang Coronation Throne and Dubdi Monastery, and it is the base for the Dzongri and Goecha La treks into Khangchendzonga National Park. It is roughly 145 km from Gangtok, so plan it as part of a West Sikkim circuit.

5. Tashiding

Tashiding Monastery sits on a hilltop above the Rathong and Rangit rivers and is considered one of Sikkim's holiest sites. The Bumchu festival is held here in late winter or early spring. The village is quiet, and the views across the valley are excellent.

6. Rinchenpong

Rinchenpong is a calm hill village near Kaluk with views of Kanchenjunga, a monastery and cardamom and orange orchards around it. It suits slow travel, birdwatching and short village walks.

7. Khecheopalri Lake

Khecheopalri is a sacred lake in a forest, known as the wish-fulfilling lake. Prayer flags hang over it, and a short forest walk leads from the car park. Visitors keep it quiet and clean, which is part of its charm. It is roughly 25 km from Pelling.

Offbeat destinations in North Sikkim

8. Dzongu

Dzongu is a protected reserve of the Lepcha community, with forest trails, waterfalls, birdlife and village homestays. A special permit is required, so plan ahead with a registered agent. It is one of the best places in Sikkim to experience local culture slowly.

9. Thangu Valley

Thangu, at around 13,000 ft, is a high valley of yak herders, with clear night skies and cold air. It is on the road to Gurudongmar Lake, and is a good place to spend a quiet evening if you want to acclimatise. A permit is needed.

10. Chopta Valley

Chopta Valley, at around 13,200 ft near Lachen, is known for wildflower meadows and glacial streams. It is best from May to June and October to November, and needs a permit.

Best season for offbeat Sikkim

March to June is good for flowers and mild weather in most places. October to December brings clear skies and sharp mountain views. Winter adds snow in the high places, which is beautiful but can limit access.

Permits and practical tips

  • Permit-needed areas (Zuluk, Nathang, Thangu, Chopta, Dzongu) are best arranged through a registered Sikkim tour operator, with a valid photo ID and passport-size photographs.
  • Rules change, so confirm current requirements before you travel.
  • Offbeat roads are slower than they look. Add buffer time to every drive.
  • Carry warm layers, cash and offline maps. Network and ATMs are limited in remote villages.
  • Homestays in small villages often have limited rooms, so book ahead.

Where to stay for offbeat Sikkim

For West Sikkim, base yourself at Silverwood Pelling, close to Khecheopalri, Tashiding and Yuksom. For the high north, Silverwood Lachen and Silverwood Lachung put you near Thangu, Chopta, Yumthang and Gurudongmar. In Gangtok, stay at The Mazong Hotel or Silverwood Heritage before you head east to Zuluk and Nathang.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best offbeat places in Sikkim?

Zuluk, Nathang Valley, Aritar (Lampokhari Lake), Dzongu, Yuksom, Tashiding, Rinchenpong and the high valleys of Thangu and Chopta are some of the best offbeat places in Sikkim.

Which offbeat place in Sikkim is best for first-time visitors?

Aritar and Rinchenpong are easy, gentle choices with views and village walks. Zuluk is the classic offbeat road trip, but it needs a permit and a longer drive.

Do offbeat places in Sikkim need permits?

Some do. Areas such as Zuluk, Nathang Valley, Thangu and Chopta in the north, and Dzongu need permits, usually arranged through a registered tour operator. Many places in West Sikkim, like Yuksom and Tashiding, do not. Always confirm current rules before you travel.

When is the best time to visit offbeat Sikkim?

March to June and October to December suit most places.

How many days do I need to explore offbeat Sikkim?

Plan 3 to 4 days for a West Sikkim circuit (Pelling, Khecheopalri, Yuksom, Tashiding) and 2 to 3 days for East Sikkim's Zuluk and Nathang route.

Are offbeat places in Sikkim crowded?

Far less than Gangtok or Pelling. Many are quiet villages and valleys, which is exactly their appeal.
