Places to Visit in and Around Gangtok: Complete Travel Guide (2026)

Gangtok, the capital of Sikkim, sits high on a ridge with Mount Kanchenjunga on the skyline. It is a compact, walkable hill town, and it is also the base for some of the best day trips in the Eastern Himalaya. If you are searching for places to visit in and around Gangtok, this guide covers the town sights, the classic day trips, and the longer journeys into North and West Sikkim, with practical tips for each.

Quick guide: Gangtok at a glance

  • Best time: late September to mid-December and March to May.
  • Time needed: 3 days for Gangtok and day trips, plus 2 to 3 days for North Sikkim or Pelling.
  • Permits: required for Tsomgo Lake and Nathula Pass (details below).
  • Getting around: the town sights are close together, so a local taxi for the day is the easiest option. Day trips are done by hired or shared vehicle.

Best time to visit Gangtok

The two best windows are late September to mid-December, when skies are clear and Kanchenjunga is at its sharpest, and March to May, when rhododendrons bloom on the hills. Winter days are cool and nights are cold. July to early September is monsoon season, with heavy rain and landslide risk on mountain roads, so it is the hardest time to travel.

Top places to visit in Gangtok town

1. Tashi View Point

Around 7 km from the town centre, Tashi View Point is one of the best places to watch the sunrise over Mount Kanchenjunga and Mount Siniolchu. Go early, carry a jacket, and expect a short climb from the car park.

2. Enchey Monastery

Enchey Monastery is a peaceful monastery about 3 km from the centre, set among pine trees. It is roughly 200 years old and is known for its annual Cham mask dance festival. Allow about 45 minutes.

3. Do Drul Chorten

This white stupa, built in 1945, is one of the most important in Sikkim. It is ringed by 108 prayer wheels, and visitors walk around it clockwise while turning them. It sits about 3 km from the centre.

4. MG Marg

MG Marg is Gangtok's pedestrian-only main street, lined with cafes, restaurants and shops. It is the best place to spend an evening, try momos and local snacks, and pick up souvenirs.

5. Gangtok Ropeway

The zig-zag cable car glides over the town in about 15 to 20 minutes and gives a bird's-eye view of the ridge, the valleys and, on clear days, the snow peaks.

6. Hanuman Tok

A temple maintained by the Indian Army at around 7,200 ft, roughly 8 km from town. It has a calm atmosphere and wide views of Kanchenjunga.

7. Ganesh Tok

A small hilltop temple dedicated to Lord Ganesha with a viewpoint over Gangtok. It is tiny, so expect to wait your turn at busy hours. Morning is best for mountain views.

8. Banjhakri Falls and Energy Park

A landscaped waterfall park about 8 km from town, with statues inspired by local folklore about the Jhakri healers. It is a good short stop, especially with kids.

9. Himalayan Zoological Park

Sikkim's first zoo, in Bulbuley, keeps animals in large natural enclosures. Red pandas, snow leopards and the Himalayan monal (the state bird) are the highlights. Check the weekly closing day before you go.

10. Namgyal Institute of Tibetology

A museum and research library about 2 km from the centre, with Tibetan art, rare manuscripts and Buddhist artefacts. It is worth an hour if you enjoy history and culture, and it is closed on some days, so check ahead.

Best day trips around Gangtok

11. Rumtek Monastery (about 22 to 24 km)

Rumtek is one of Sikkim's largest monasteries and the main seat of the Kagyu lineage of Tibetan Buddhism. It is known for its golden stupa, murals and woodwork, and visitors can watch the monks at prayer. The drive takes about an hour each way.

12. Tsomgo (Changu) Lake (about 38 km)

A glacial lake at roughly 12,400 ft whose colour shifts with the seasons, often frozen in winter and bright blue in summer. Yak rides are available nearby. A permit is required, and the road can close in bad weather.

13. Baba Harbhajan Singh Mandir

A shrine beyond Tsomgo Lake built in memory of Sepoy Harbhajan Singh, whom the Indian Army remembers with great respect. Many travellers stop here on the way to Nathula Pass.

14. Nathula Pass

Nathula is a high mountain pass at the India-China border, at around 14,000 ft. Most travellers combine Tsomgo Lake, Baba Mandir and Nathula into one long day trip.

  • Permit: required. Indian tourists usually get it through a registered travel agent or the Sikkim tourism department, with a photo ID and passport-size photographs.
  • Closed days: generally Mondays and Tuesdays.
  • Best season: April to mid-June and October to November. Avoid peak monsoon.
  • Health tip: the altitude is high, so go slowly, drink water and do not rush.

Rules, fees and timings change, so confirm them with a registered agent or the official tourism department before you travel.

Beyond Gangtok: longer journeys from the capital

If you have more time, these destinations are the natural next steps. Each is roughly 5 to 6 hours by road from Gangtok, depending on weather and road conditions.

Lachung and Yumthang Valley

Lachung in North Sikkim is the gateway to Yumthang Valley, the valley of flowers, and to Zero Point at around 15,000 ft, where snow stays through much of the year. Silverwood Lachung is our property in the village.

Lachen and Gurudongmar Lake

Lachen is the base for Gurudongmar Lake, one of the highest lakes in India at above 17,000 ft. Start before sunrise and expect a cold, thin-air morning. Silverwood Lachen is our property in the village.

Pelling

West Sikkim's Pelling offers close views of Kanchenjunga, Pemayangtse Monastery, Khecheopalri Lake and the Skywalk. Silverwood Pelling is our property there.

Sample 3-day Gangtok itinerary

  1. Day 1, town sights: Tashi View Point at sunrise, Enchey Monastery, Do Drul Chorten, the ropeway, then an evening on MG Marg.
  2. Day 2, Nathula day trip: Tsomgo Lake, Baba Mandir and Nathula Pass (not on Monday or Tuesday). Start early and return by evening.
  3. Day 3, culture and views: Rumtek Monastery in the morning, then Hanuman Tok, Ganesh Tok and Banjhakri Falls.

Practical tips for visiting Gangtok

  • Carry warm layers year-round, since evenings are cool even in summer.
  • Book permits at least a day ahead for Tsomgo Lake and Nathula, and carry photo ID.
  • Keep a flexible plan in monsoon months in case roads close.
  • Start day trips early. Mornings have the clearest mountain views.
  • Check opening days for museums, the zoo and monasteries before you head out.

Frequently asked questions

What are the best places to visit in Gangtok?

The most popular places are Tashi View Point, Enchey Monastery, Do Drul Chorten, MG Marg, the Gangtok ropeway, Hanuman Tok, Ganesh Tok, Banjhakri Falls and the Himalayan Zoological Park. Most can be covered in one or two days.

What are the best day trips from Gangtok?

Rumtek Monastery, Tsomgo (Changu) Lake, Baba Harbhajan Singh Mandir and Nathula Pass are the classic day trips. Tsomgo, Baba Mandir and Nathula are usually combined into one long day.

Do I need a permit to visit Tsomgo Lake and Nathula Pass?

Yes. Indian tourists need a permit for Tsomgo Lake and Nathula, which is usually arranged through a registered travel agent or the Sikkim tourism department, with a photo ID and passport-size photographs. Nathula is generally closed to visitors on Mondays and Tuesdays, so check current rules before you plan.

What is the best time to visit Gangtok?

Late September to mid-December and March to May are the best months. Autumn brings clear mountain views and spring brings rhododendron blooms. July to early September is monsoon season, when landslides can disrupt roads.

How many days are enough for Gangtok?

Plan 3 days for Gangtok and its day trips. Add 2 to 3 days if you also want to visit North Sikkim (Lachen and Lachung) or Pelling.

How far are Lachung, Lachen and Pelling from Gangtok?

Lachung and Lachen in North Sikkim and Pelling in West Sikkim are each roughly 5 to 6 hours from Gangtok by road, depending on weather and road conditions.
